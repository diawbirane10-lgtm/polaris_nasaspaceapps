# POLARIS — NASA Space Apps 2026 Project Page (Draft)

**Challenge:** Lunar Landing Site Navigator (10 – CLPS)

## High-Level Summary
POLARIS is an open-source lunar south-pole mission decision tool that turns NASA planetary data into understandable site-and-date comparisons. When a landing is delayed, POLARIS compares the original observation site, the postponed schedule and an alternative site, showing when Sun and Earth are locally visible and how a simplified power-storage model responds to changing assumptions.

Rather than claiming a single “best” landing site, POLARIS exposes physical trade-offs and clearly labels incomplete terrain coverage and uncertain communications. Its operator dashboard lets learners and engineers examine real NASA datasets with traceable scientific evidence.

## Challenge response
Near the lunar south pole, low solar elevations and complex mountains create extreme shadows, while a small change in time or location alters Earth visibility. POLARIS uses NASA data to make these comparisons quick, interactive and interpretable for mission planners, educators and the public.

## What the working prototype does
- Compares two sample observation coordinates in the south polar region, with provenance-tracked LOLA topography and JPL Horizons apparent Sun/Earth azimuth and elevation at six-hour samples.
- Estimates a 6-km local terrain skyline around each site using 5-m/pixel NASA LOLA DEM and lunar curvature.
- Supports a 24-hour nominal mission, a 48-hour delay and the alternate site at the delayed time, plus modeled PV/load stress tests.
- Visualizes official NASA Moon Trek south-pole WMTS aggregate illumination/Earth-visibility layers separately from the time-specific model.
- Provides a direct visual reconnaissance link to LROC QuickMap and machine-readable source/validation reports.
- Independently checks ephemeris angles with NASA NAIF SPICE (228 angular comparisons, two sites, 14 days). The stored 4-day sample can be rerun using provided scripts.

## Limitations (must be shown to the jury)
- This is an engineering demonstrator, **not a certified landing-site selector**.
- Local horizon calculations stop at 6 km; distant terrain may block additional sunlight or direct-to-Earth line of sight.
- Earth-above-local-skyline estimates **are not radio-link availability**. Actual link budget, antenna pointing and terrestrial ground stations are not modeled.
- Sampling every six hours may miss illumination or visibility transitions, and produces approximate window lengths.
- Simplified power model excludes detailed thermal, radiation, mechanical, array pointing and energy-system aging effects.
- Custom-coordinate live Horizons requests use a geometric horizon and are **not terrain-aware**.

## Scientific validation
The NAIF SPICE `spkcpo` model was evaluated using Moon-fixed surface observer coordinates and the `LT+S` aberration mode, with `de440s.bsp`, a lunar binary PCK, lunar frame definitions and a leap-seconds kernel. SPICE was compared with NASA/JPL Horizons observer azimuth and elevation (DE441, airless). Across 57 epochs for each of Sun and Earth at two sites, the maximum angular disagreement was approximately 0.0000011°. This confirms consistency of the sampled geometric calculations, **not** terrain-model accuracy or mission feasibility.

## AI tools disclosure
ChatGPT was used for implementation assistance, code generation, quality checks and documentation. AI-generated code and documentation are human-reviewable and included with test cases. NASA source data are not generated by AI. Numerical geometry verification used NASA NAIF SPICE rather than AI estimates.

## Demonstration for judges (target: under 30 seconds)
1. Open the POLARIS live site. Explain: “The lunar south pole challenges landers with very low sunlight and uncertain views of Earth.”
2. Select the NASA reference dataset for Connecting Ridge or Shackleton Rim. Show the Sun/Earth horizon plot and open the Moon Trek atlas as context (aggregated, not date-specific).
3. Open “Mission Reschedule Lab” and click “SIMULATE +48H DELAY.” Compare the baseline, delayed original site and delayed alternate site. Note why an apparent visibility opportunity is only a local upper bound.
4. Open the SPICE verification report, say the team used 228 numerical cross-checks, and point to the documented limitations.
